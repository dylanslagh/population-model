\section{Discussion}

The present framework is designed to expose structural disagreement rather
than make long-run uncertainty look smaller than it is. That transparency also
clarifies several limitations.

First, WPP estimates are model-based comparison data, particularly where census
and registration systems are weak. Second, country-level paths to 2150 can be
dominated by migration assumptions that are less stable than world totals.
Third, converting total fertility and life expectancy into age schedules adds
uncertainty that should be tested rather than hidden. Fourth, social-group
behavior may change as groups grow, so persistence estimated from small groups
cannot be extrapolated mechanically. Finally, a detailed mechanism can mislead
more effectively than a transparent trend model if its parameters are not
independently constrained.

Mortality deserves similar restraint. \citet{oeppen2002} document sustained
increases in record female life expectancy despite repeatedly proposed
ceilings, but that observation does not identify one correct age-specific
mortality path through 2150. Long-run results will therefore remain conditional
on named mortality and extension policies.

The interactive webpage is intended to become a public companion to the
analysis, not evidence in itself. It should make assumptions and uncertainty
explorable while the paper provides durable methods, limitations, and, after
release, citable results.
