\section*{How this paper was made}
\label{sec:contributions}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{How this paper was made}

Two of the three authors are language models. We list them because they did the
work that authorship normally denotes, and because a paper arguing that a
projection should be auditable would be in a poor position to obscure how it was
itself produced.

\textbf{Dylan Slagh} conceived the project, set its scope and its constraints,
made every decision about what the model would and would not attempt, chose the
questions the paper answers and the order it answers them in, and reviewed all
of it. The standing rules the work is held to --- in particular that no
mechanism parameter may be fitted to the series it explains, and that a stored
prediction may never be overwritten --- are enforced throughout at his
insistence.

\textbf{Claude Opus 5} built the mechanism layer, the migration and rate
continuations, the uncertainty analyses and the reproducibility machinery, and
wrote this manuscript.

\textbf{ChatGPT 5.6 Sol} did earlier model and specification work, and wrote an
adversarial review of the first complete draft. Several of its objections
changed the paper's claims rather than its wording: the distinction between
cancelling a terminal fertility rate and cancelling an accumulated population
(\Cref{sec:boundary}), the correction that a constructed covariance is not an
observed one (\Cref{sec:response}), and the removal of an unsupported
explanation for cross-country variation in the mechanism's strength.

The conversation record that produced this work is released with it: eleven
conversations, from the one that designed the project to the one that revised
this manuscript. It is not a tidy document, and that is the point of releasing
it. The errors it contains were caught by the checks described in
\Cref{sec:engine} and in the supplement, and at least one of them is visible
arriving --- the absurdity check that expected roughly 244 billion people under
constant fertility, which the supplement records as wrong, was offered in passing
in the first conversation and hardened into a stated requirement before the
engine was built and disagreed with it. A reader who wants to know how much to
trust this paper is entitled to see how many times it was wrong first.

Current journal authorship policies generally do not admit non-human authors.
This author line therefore describes the division of labour rather than making a
claim on credit as those policies define it; the human author takes
responsibility for the whole of it.
