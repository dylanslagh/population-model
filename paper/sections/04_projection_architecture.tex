\section{Projection architecture}

\subsection{A deterministic accounting layer}

The population state is indexed by country, sex, and single year of age from
zero through an open-ended 100-plus group. Counts refer to 1 January. One model
step advances the state to the following 1 January by applying survival into
each age, computing births from mid-year female exposure and age-specific
fertility, splitting births by sex ratio, and applying net migration.

For location $l$, sex $s$, and closed age $a$, the aging operation is
schematically
\begin{equation}
  N_{l,s,a,t+1} = S_{l,s,a,t} N_{l,s,a-1,t} + M_{l,s,a,t},
\end{equation}
with separate treatment of births and the open age group. Births are computed
from fertility rates $f_{l,a,t}$ and the average of beginning- and end-of-year
female exposure. Childbearing ages span 10--54 so the implementation retains
the nonzero rates reported in the published WPP inputs outside ages 15--49.

This module contains no account of future fertility. That is a design
constraint: if a behavioral assumption enters the accounting layer, later
results can no longer be attributed cleanly to the mechanism that generated
them.

\subsection{Implementation validation}

The engine is checked against WPP 2024 results using published inputs rather
than fitted corrections. The primary validation uses zero migration because
all required terms are independently available. A medium-path migration term
backed out of the UN trajectory is retained only as a diagnostic: it absorbs
differences between implementations and is not independent evidence.

Validation is performed at world, country, age-group, open-age, and
constant-fertility levels. Passing these checks establishes implementation
fidelity to the selected accounting conventions. It does not validate any
substantive assumption about future fertility or mortality.

The zero-migration comparison is the primary independent-input accounting
test. The constant-fertility comparison is a secondary check and inherits the
derived migration residual used to reproduce the corresponding WPP variant.

\subsection{Two-stage probabilistic propagation}

The sampler and the population engine remain separate. Statistical models or
source adapters first produce complete draws of demographic rates. Each draw is
then advanced through the deterministic engine one at a time. This follows the
general architecture of probabilistic population projection
\citep{raftery2012,sevcikova2016} while keeping the deterministic installation
lightweight and the provenance of each component visible.
