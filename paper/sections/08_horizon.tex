\section{The horizon, and whose numbers are whose}
\label{sec:horizon}

A projection running to \ProjectionEndYear{} on inputs that stop at 2100 has a
problem of attribution rather than of arithmetic. Everything past the last year
of published assumptions is the modeller's own extrapolation, and drawn as one
continuous line the reader cannot see where the source ended.

\subsection{Three separate objects}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=nextline]
  \item[The UN reproduction.] World Population Prospects 2024's medium path,
  reproduced by this engine, through \OfficialBoundaryYear{} and no further. No
  value after that year is labelled a UN projection.
  \item[The project extension.] Begins from the published population on 1
  January \OfficialBoundaryYear{} and runs to \ProjectionEndYear{} with
  stochastic continuations of the published WPP 2024 fertility and mortality
  trajectories, together with stochastic migration. Because the public archives
  do not include the fitted posterior state, these are emulators of the source
  trajectories rather than an official United Nations projection.
  \item[The selection model.] A separate object forking in
  \PhaseFiveBaseYear{}, so that selection acts over the whole projection rather
  than switching on after the UN's assumptions have already fixed the
  \OfficialBoundaryYear{} population.
\end{description}

\subsection{What happens after the source stops}
\label{sec:post-2100}

Holding final rates constant after 2100 assumes that fertility ceases to vary and
longevity ceases to improve exactly at the source boundary. Because this
assumption governs fifty of the projection's 126 years, it is evaluated against
a continuation of the source's late-horizon behaviour. Fitted over
\ExtensionFitStart{}--\ExtensionFitEnd{}, the two components exhibit different
dynamics, which the continuation treats separately.

\textbf{Fertility is stationary within a trajectory.} Each published path
fluctuates around its own level with an autocorrelation near 0.89, and a
stationary spread of about 0.12 children. The spread \emph{between} trajectories
at 2100 is roughly 0.40 --- three times larger. That is the published model's
structure: the long-run level is itself uncertain, and most of the fertility
uncertainty at \ProjectionEndYear{} is already present at the boundary. Each path
is therefore continued as an autoregression around \emph{its own} 2100 value.
Pooling across trajectories would instead fit one level per country and draw
every trajectory toward it, substantially reducing the posterior spread.

\textbf{Longevity is still trending.} The annual gain in female life expectancy
over \ExtensionFitStart{}--\ExtensionFitEnd{} averages
\ContinuationEZeroDrift{} years and is strikingly uniform across countries.
Freezing it asserts that improvement halts at the boundary. Each path is
continued as a random walk with its country's own fitted drift plus
autoregressive noise on the gain, with the male series derived from the female
series and a separately continued sex gap so the two cannot cross.

Two limits are stated rather than buried. Fertility is still drifting downward
slightly at the boundary, and centring each path on its 2100 value drops that
residual drift rather than extrapolating it; extrapolating would subtract roughly
0.15 children by \ProjectionEndYear{}, so this choice biases the result
\emph{upward}. And the continuation does not reproduce the published mortality
model's deceleration, in which gains slow as life expectancy rises, so it
slightly overstates longevity in the same direction. Both are emulators of a model's
output, not continuations of the published model, for the same reason as the
migration continuation: the public archive omits the fitted posterior state. The
age \emph{patterns} --- the shape of fertility across ages, and the mortality
standard the levels are scaled onto --- are still held at their final published
shape, which is a much weaker assumption than holding a level and is stated
separately for that reason.

\subsection{Sensitivity to post-2100 continuation}

Holding rates constant after 2100 gives a
\ProjectionEndYear{} world median of \ComparatorTwentyOneFifty{} billion with a
90\% range of \ComparatorTwentyOneFiftyLow{} to
\ComparatorTwentyOneFiftyHigh{}. Continuing the source's own behaviour gives
\ContinuedTwentyOneFifty{} billion with a range of \ContinuedLow{} to
\ContinuedHigh{}, a median shift of \HoldVsContinueMedian{} billion and a change
in width of \HoldVsContinueWidth{} billion.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig6-fan.pdf}
\caption{Uncertainty accumulating. \textbf{(a)} World population under the
conventional mean-reverting posterior propagated through this engine,
\ComparatorDraws{} draws, with fertility and longevity continued past 2100 rather
than frozen. Shaded bands are the central 50\% and 90\% of draws. The fan widens
throughout because small differences in long-run fertility compound through both
cohort size and later births, and it keeps widening after 2100 rather than
stopping at an artificial boundary. \textbf{(b)} Medians only, against the
version holding rates constant after 2100, with the selection model's benchmark
and stress-test paths for scale. \textbf{(c)} The width of the 90\% band year by
year. The two are nearly indistinguishable, which is the finding: the
frozen-rate assumption moved the centre of the distribution without much
affecting how fast it opened.}
\label{fig:fan}
\end{figure}

Continuing the source moves the median
\emph{up}, for the two reasons stated above --- longevity keeps improving and the
residual fertility drift is not extrapolated --- while the extra fifty years of
fertility innovation widen the band only modestly, because the published
fertility process is stationary within a trajectory and most of the eventual
spread is already there at 2100.

\subsection{Which uncertainty dominates depends on where you look}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig5-decomposition.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{(a)} One-at-a-time sensitivity: the 90\% width of world
population in \ProjectionEndYear{} when one source is varied across its own draws
and the others are held at a median trajectory. These are not commensurable in
the way a variance decomposition would be --- fertility, mortality and migration
are posterior draws, while the mechanism entry is a uniform sweep over
judgemental ranges --- so the comparison is of magnitude, not of attributable
share. \textbf{(b)} The same comparison inside individual countries at 2100,
where the ordering reverses.}
\label{fig:decomposition}
\end{figure}

Varying one source at a time across \DecompositionDraws{} draws
(\Cref{fig:decomposition}a), the 90\% width of world population in
\ProjectionEndYear{} is \WidthFertility{} billion for long-run fertility,
\WidthMechanism{} billion for the selection mechanism's parameters,
\WidthMortality{} billion for mortality and \WidthMigration{} billion for
migration. The mechanism entry is not directly comparable with the others: it
varies judgemental ranges against the UN's medium demographic rates rather than
propagating a posterior, and \Cref{sec:results} explains why its band is a
sensitivity envelope. It is included to place uncertainty in the mechanism's
parameters on the same scale as the demographic inputs.

The country panel (\Cref{fig:decomposition}b) reverses the ordering in the world
panel. At 2100, migration uncertainty is
\MigrationOverFertilityUae{} times fertility uncertainty for the United Arab
Emirates and \MigrationOverFertilityCanada{} times for Canada, but only
\MigrationOverFertilityNigeria{} times for Nigeria. In the project extension the
Emirates' \ProjectionEndYear{} population runs from \UaeExtensionLow{} million to
\UaeExtensionHigh{} million around a median of \UaeExtensionMedian{} million ---
a factor of more than two hundred, for a component contributing almost nothing to
the world total. Any single summary of ``the'' uncertainty in a population
projection is answering a question about the world that most users of projections
are not asking.

\subsection{A conventional comparator}
\label{sec:comparator}

The published Bayesian posterior for fertility and mortality
\citep{alkema2011,raftery2012,sevcikova2016bayespop} was propagated through this
same engine one draw at a time: \ComparatorDraws{} draws,
\ComparatorCountries{} countries, to \ProjectionEndYear{}. This is the
conventional mean-reverting view rather than the compositional specification. It
is stored as an immutable dated record and marked as a comparator rather than as
a claim of this paper.

As an external consistency check, the separate deterministic run on the UN's
published assumptions peaks at
\UnEquivalentPeak{} billion in \UnEquivalentPeakYear{}, against the ensemble
median's \ComparatorPeak{} billion in \ComparatorPeakYear{}. Those two
calculations share only the engine, and they agree on the peak to within a fifth
of a billion people and on its date to within a decade.
