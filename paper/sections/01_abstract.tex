\begin{abstract}
Conditional on age-specific fertility, mortality, and migration schedules,
cohort-component projection is deterministic. Long-run results nevertheless
depend on uncertain structural assumptions about how those schedules evolve.
This research protocol develops a reproducible framework that keeps those two
layers separate. It first
backtests observable portions of archived United Nations population projection
vintages against WPP 2024 estimates. It then validates a
single-year cohort-component engine against published \DataVintage{} projection
inputs and outputs. The next stage will pass probabilistic fertility and
mortality trajectories through that same engine before comparing explicit
accounts of post-transition fertility. The intended contribution is not a
single preferred population total for \ProjectionEndYear{}. It is a framework
in which competing explanations use identical demographic bookkeeping, expose
their assumptions, preserve dated predictions, and identify nearer-term
outcomes by which they can later be rejected or revised. Probabilistic and
mechanism-comparison results are forthcoming and are not claimed here.
\end{abstract}
