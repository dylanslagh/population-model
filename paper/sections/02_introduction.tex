\section{Introduction}

Population projections are often discussed as if disagreement centered on the
arithmetic. At long horizons, it does not. Once age-specific fertility,
mortality, and migration rates have been supplied, cohort-component projection
is deterministic bookkeeping. The scientifically consequential uncertainty is
upstream: what processes generate those rates, especially after fertility has
fallen below replacement.

This distinction matters more as the horizon lengthens. By
\ProjectionEndYear{}, nearly everyone alive will have been born after the
\ProjectionBaseYear{} base year. Small but persistent
differences in fertility therefore compound through both cohort size and later
births. Mortality and migration remain important, but neither removes the need
to state why long-run fertility should stabilize, rebound, or continue to fall.

The United Nations now produces deterministic and probabilistic population
projections using a cohort-component engine and Bayesian trajectories for the
components of demographic change \citep{un2024methodology}. The underlying
probabilistic framework is well established
\citep{alkema2011,raftery2012,raftery2023}. The contribution is not a new
cohort-component engine, but a transparent comparison of alternative
rate-generating models through a common validated accounting layer, with
predictions preserved in a form that can be scored later.

Three questions will organize the completed study:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item What signed and regional error patterns recur in observable portions of
  past World Population Prospects (WPP) projection vintages?
  \item How much do long-run outcomes change when alternative fertility
  mechanisms are passed through identical demographic accounting?
  \item Which nearer-term cohort outcomes distinguish those mechanisms soon
  enough to provide a meaningful test?
\end{enumerate}

The final question is central. Because a 2150 endpoint cannot be evaluated
within a useful horizon, the study pre-specifies nearer-term cohort outcomes
that distinguish the competing mechanisms. The workflow therefore treats
prior predictive checking, retrospective testing, and future scorekeeping as
part of the research design rather than presentation added afterward
\citep{gelman2013,gneiting2007}.
