\section{The engine, in full}
\label{app:engine}

\subsection{Validation against published outputs}

The engine is a standard cohort-component projection: single year of age 0--100
with an open-ended final group, two sexes, 237 countries and territories, annual
steps, populations dated 1 January, rates in force during the calendar year. It
takes fertility, survival and net migration as given and does the bookkeeping,
with no theory about the future inside it.

The typed engine of \S2 is the same code with a composition axis. With every
propensity set to one it reproduces the ordinary engine to a relative difference
of $3\times10^{-16}$, and one generation of it matches the analytic
parent--offspring covariance response to nine decimal places.

\subsection{Implementation conventions}
\label{app:conventions}

The following source conventions are enforced explicitly because each can
produce a numerically plausible but biased projection if misinterpreted.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item Ages run 0--100 where 100 is an open-ended group; sex 0 is female.
\item Survival $s[a]$ is survival \emph{into} age $a$, following the source's own
convention, so no translation step stands between the published table and the
engine. $s[0]$ is birth survival, and $s[100]$ applies to the sum of the
99-year-olds and the existing 100+ group.
\item Populations are people. The source publishes thousands; the multiplication
happens exactly once, at ingest.
\item Fertility is births per woman per year. The source publishes per thousand.
\item Childbearing ages are 10--54. The single-age source file stops at 49 and
the five-year file does not; ages 50--54 account for about 0.3\% of world births
and are included from the five-year file.
\item Age-specific fertility uses mid-year women, so the denominator is the mean
of the 1 January figure and the following one. Getting this wrong biases births
by roughly half a year of cohort change --- negligible in one year, not
negligible after 126.
\item The United Nations' constant-fertility variant freezes 2023, not 2024:
about 0.6\% higher than the 2024 medium rates, compounding to 2.7\% by 2100. The
comparison reads the UN's own frozen rates rather than assuming ours.
\item World quantiles are computed from each draw's own world total, never by
summing country quantiles. Adding every country's 5th percentile assumes all 236
land in their own bad tail in the same draw; at \ProjectionEndYear{} that
shortcut gives 3.13 billion where the correct world 5th percentile is
\ComparatorTwentyOneFiftyLow{} billion.
\item Archived WPP releases ship counterfactual scenarios beside the main
projection --- ``no AIDS'', ``instant replacement'', ``zero migration''. They are
excluded by name so that the backtest evaluates intended projections rather than
counterfactual exercises.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Migration after 2100}

The published Bayesian migration trajectories \citep{azose2015} run to 2100 and
the public archive does not include the fitted posterior state, so the
continuation refits the same autoregressive form to the
\ExtensionFitStart{}--\ExtensionFitEnd{} portion of those trajectories and
continues each path from its own \OfficialBoundaryYear{} rate. This is an
emulator of a model's output, not an official continuation.

Every draw-year is balanced so that world net migration is exactly zero, by
shifting active-country rates by a common amount, which redistributes the
discrepancy in proportion to current populations. The United Nations uses a more
detailed staged procedure; this implements the essential accounting constraint
and is labelled an approximation. Where a schedule would remove more people from
a narrow age and sex cell than exist, the unmet removal is redistributed across
populated cells rather than clipped, because clipping silently creates people.

The resulting extension gives \ExtensionTwentyOneHundred{} billion in
\OfficialBoundaryYear{}, \ExtensionTwentyOneTwentyFive{} billion in 2125, and
\ExtensionTwentyOneFifty{} billion in \ProjectionEndYear{}, with a
migration-only 90\% range of \ExtensionTwentyOneFiftyLow{} to
\ExtensionTwentyOneFiftyHigh{} billion.

\subsection{Fertility and longevity after 2100}
\label{app:rates}

Fitted parameters for the continuation described in \S7.2, over
\ExtensionFitStart{}--\ExtensionFitEnd{}: the within-trajectory fertility
autocorrelation has a median of \ContinuationTfrPhi{} with a median innovation
spread of \ContinuationTfrSigma{} children, and the mean annual gain in female
life expectancy is \ContinuationEZeroDrift{} years, ranging from
\ContinuationEZeroDriftLow{} to \ContinuationEZeroDriftHigh{} across countries.

Continued values are clipped only when they leave the full range occupied by the
source archive, padded by one quarter of its width. Clipping affects
\ContinuationClippedShare{} of generated values. These source-derived rails
permit the observed female--male life-expectancy gap of $-6.6$ to $+20.2$ years
and prevent ordinary source behaviour from being altered by an implicit model.
