Abstract

Conditional on age-specific fertility, mortality, and migration schedules, cohort-component projection is deterministic. Long-run results nevertheless depend on uncertain structural assumptions about how those schedules evolve. This project separates those layers, backtests archived United Nations projection vintages, validates an independent demographic engine, and defines a framework in which competing explanations of post-transition fertility can be compared through identical accounting and scored against nearer-term outcomes.

Data and versions

The preliminary analysis uses WPP 2024 as its comparison and base-data vintage. The probabilistic source archives are University of Washington products aligned to WPP 2024, not official UN posterior draws. Their exact byte lengths and SHA-256 fingerprints are pinned in data/manifest/uw_wpp2024_files.json.

Paper version and model-data vintage are kept separate: a prose revision does not silently become a new forecast, and a new forecast does not overwrite an older paper.
