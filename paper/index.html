Selection on fertility, and the environmental decline that would cancel it

A compositional mechanism in a 237-country cohort-component projection to 2150.

The argument in short

Long-run population projections model the national fertility rate as a time series that reverts toward a long-run level. That representation has no composition in it. But completed family size varies widely within every population, and it is transmitted imperfectly from parents to children, so the next generation is drawn disproportionately from the larger families. That is selection, its direction on fertility is upward, and it is absent from the standard projection machinery by construction.

This paper adds a composition axis to a cohort-component engine that reproduces the United Nations' own published projection to 0.001% of world population at 2100. With every fertility propensity set to one, the extended engine reproduces the ordinary one to machine precision — so any difference in the results is the mechanism, not a second implementation of the arithmetic.

Two contributors named in the byline are language models. The paper includes a contribution and responsibility statement identifying their roles. The public conversation record provides optional process documentation; the paper and supplement contain the full argument, methods and reproducibility information needed by readers.

The findings

Selection is real but modest. It raises world population in 2150 from 8.54 to 10.36 billion, and lifts observed fertility 17.4% above the environment path that produced it. It shifts the level of the long-run curve; it does not restore growth.

The best-documented high-fertility groups are a small part of it. The Haredi and Amish populations, which dominate popular discussion of this mechanism, contribute 2.5% of what unlabelled variation inside ordinary populations contributes — even though the model is generous to them at every choice point.

The headline result is a threshold, not a number. An additional uniform decline in the fertility environment of 1.52% per decade after 2050 cancels selection's effect on the fertility rate by 2150; cancelling its effect on the 2150 population takes 2.45%, because the extra people already produced are still alive. Across the empirical range of the two measured parameters the first threshold runs from 0.33% to 4.49% per decade.

The decisive quantity in a 150-year projection — how the conditions of parenthood keep changing — cannot be measured in advance. Assigning it by judgement would allow an assumption to determine the headline result. Reporting the threshold instead expresses that assumption as a rate that could in principle be observed.

What the paper does not claim

It is not a forecast

Every comparison is conditional on a stated path. The paper reports where the model's answer changes sign, not what the answer is.

It is not a claim about genes

The transmission parameter is an observed parent–child correlation in realised family size, combining whatever genetic, material and cultural continuity exists. No part of the argument requires separating them.

It is not an economic model

The fertility environment is a stated multiplier path, not a projection of income, housing or childcare costs.

Five of thirteen parameters are unmeasured

They are labelled scenario knobs, the loader refuses to let one claim to be verified, and none is allowed to carry a headline result.

Reproducibility

No headline number in the paper is typed into the prose. Every value in the text, tables and figures is written by a script into a machine-readable result file and from there into a LaTeX macro or a figure; the generator refuses to build if a result file is missing, if an expected key is absent, or if a value has moved outside a stated sanity bound.

Every downloaded source file is checksummed and its manifest committed, so a source that is silently reissued fails loudly rather than changing a result. Two data sets are fetched rather than redistributed: the cohort fertility tabulations, whose terms do not permit republication, and the large trajectory archives. The scripts, checksums and derived statistics for both are committed.

Projections meant to be scored later are written as immutable dated records; the writer raises rather than overwrites an existing one and has no override flag. The conventional mean-reverting comparator is marked as a comparator rather than as a claim of this paper.
