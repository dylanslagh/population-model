A 237-country demographic model · working paper v1.2.1

Population forecasts forget who has the children.

Families differ in size, and part of that difference persists between generations. A national fertility average cannot represent the resulting change in population composition. This project puts that missing mechanism inside a full cohort-component model.

The finding

Selection is large enough to matter, and small enough to cancel.

The stable-low benchmark reaches 8.54 billion people in 2150. Adding ordinary variation in family size and its measured intergenerational persistence raises the same model to 10.36 billion. That is a 21% increase, without assuming a fertility rebound.

Why the average misses it

Fertility has a distribution, not just a mean.

Among 19 low-fertility countries with completed cohort data—29.1 million women—the median coefficient of variation in completed family size is 0.57. The model combines that measured spread with a parent-child correlation of 0.15.

The useful answer

How much decline would cancel selection?

Rather than choose the future fertility environment, the model solves for the point where its effect exactly offsets selection. At the benchmark, an additional universal decline of 1.52% per decade after 2050 cancels selection in the fertility rate.

1.52% per decade 1.51%–1.53% across 50 paired migration paths

Present evidence does not establish which side of this boundary the world is on. It does turn a vague disagreement about the far future into a rate that can be observed.
